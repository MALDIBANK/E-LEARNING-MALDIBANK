# Sample Preparation for Yeast and Filamentous Fungi

## Using solid culture media

### Culture

- Culture the strains on a suitable solid culture medium (Petri dishes) at optimal growth temperature (usually 25–28°C). Seed the agar surface at several points to maximize growth.
- Incubate for 2 days (yeast) or 3 days (filamentous fungi) at 25°C. If the growth is too limited after 3 days, extend the incubation until sufficient material is available to perform an extraction.

### Protein extraction

1. Prepare pre-labelled 1.5 ml microtubes containing 300 µl of sterile water, one tube per culture.
2. Using sterile forceps or loops, gently scrape the surface of the culture to collect yeast cells (or spores and mycelium for filamentous fungi), taking care not to remove any culture medium. Aim to collect an amount roughly the size of a lentil (young material from the outer edge of the colony).
3. Transfer the collected material into the microtube containing sterile water.
4. Vortex the tube shortly and add 900 µl of absolute ethanol.
5. Vortex gently and centrifuge at 13,000 g for 10 minutes (this time can be reduced — the goal is a pellet that won't fall apart in the next step).
6. Remove the supernatant by pipetting or decanting.
7. Centrifuge again briefly (1–2 minutes) at 13,000 g.
8. Remove the residual supernatant by pipetting and let the pellet air-dry for approximately 5–15 minutes.

> **Note:** the pellet should be dried until no excess drops are visible on the tube surface. A damp pellet is acceptable. Too much residual ethanol reduces extraction efficiency through dilution; an overdried pellet becomes solid and hard to resuspend in formic acid, which also reduces efficiency.

9. Once the pellet is dried, add 50 µl of 70% formic acid, vortex gently, and incubate for a maximum of 5 minutes at room temperature.
10. Vortex and add 50 µl of acetonitrile, vortex gently, and incubate for a maximum of 5 minutes at room temperature.

> **Note:** you can vary the volume of formic acid and acetonitrile (e.g. 30 µl instead of 50 µl for a more concentrated extract), but the formic acid volume must always equal the acetonitrile volume. A very small pellet needs 10–20 µl; a large pellet can need up to 80 µl. The added volume should just cover the pellet.

> **Troubleshooting:** if spectra acquisition is repeatedly unsuccessful, bead beating can be performed during the formic acid incubation.

11. Centrifuge for 2 minutes at 13,000 g. The supernatant is the final extract. Store the extract at **-20°C** until further analysis.

## Using liquid culture media (filamentous fungi)

### Culture

- Inoculate the liquid cultivation tubes (e.g. Sabouraud Dextrose Broth) with the biological material and close the lid. For sporulating fungi, use a wetted cotton swab to collect spores by touching the sporulating surface once. For non-sporulating fungi, collect a small amount of young mycelium.
- Place the tubes in the rotator incubator and incubate at 25°C overnight, or until sufficient biological material is observed. Liquid cultivation is faster than solid — one to two days is usually enough.

### Protein extraction

1. Remove the cultivation tubes from the rotator, place them on the bench, and let the biological material settle for 1–60 minutes depending on the species.
2. Harvest 1500 µl from the sediment into a pre-labelled 1.5 ml microtube using a disposable Pasteur pipette.
3. Centrifuge for 2 minutes at 13,000 g and remove the supernatant with a micropipette.

> **Note:** centrifuge an extra 3 minutes if the pellet isn't well defined. If that doesn't help, remove as much medium as possible, add 100–300 µl ethanol to improve pellet formation, centrifuge again for 3 minutes, and remove the supernatant. If the pellet is too small but the supernatant is clear, add more biological material and repeat.

4. Add 1 ml of ultrapure water, vortex, and centrifuge for 2 minutes at 13,000 g.
5. Carefully remove the supernatant, add another 1 ml of ultrapure water, and vortex to resuspend the pellet.
6. Centrifuge for 2 minutes at 13,000 g and repeat the previous water-wash step.
7. Centrifuge for 2 minutes at 13,000 g and remove the supernatant.
8. Add 300 µl of ultrapure water to the pellet and resuspend by pipetting up and down.
9. Add 900 µl of ethanol and pipette up and down.
10. Centrifuge for 2 minutes at 13,000 g, remove the supernatant, then centrifuge again briefly (1–2 minutes) and remove any residual supernatant.
11. Let the pellet air-dry for approximately 5–15 minutes (same drying guidance as above — a damp pellet is fine, an overdried one is not).
12. Add 50 µl of 70% formic acid, vortex, incubate up to 5 minutes; then add 50 µl of acetonitrile, vortex, incubate up to 5 minutes (same volume-adjustment rule as above).
13. Centrifuge for 2 minutes at 13,000 g. The supernatant is the final extract. Store at **-20°C** until further analysis.
